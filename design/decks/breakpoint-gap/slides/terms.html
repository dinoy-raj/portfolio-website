Five words for every design review, instead of “phone” and “tablet”

⏱ 85s

Retire the words “phone” and “tablet” in design reviews. On Android, an unfolded Pixel Fold in split-screen has the exact same width as a compact phone. Throughout this talk, remember what these five words mean.

▶ CLICK — Window: the viewport allocated to your app, not the physical screen. A tablet might have a large display, but your app could receive only half of it because of split-screen multitasking.

▶ CLICK — Size class: a classification of the available window width and height that tells us which layout applies — Compact, Medium, Expanded, Large and Extra-large.

▶ CLICK — Pane: a self-contained column of related content.

▶ CLICK — Hinge: the physical fold or seam between the two halves of a foldable display. Some hinges are only a visible crease; others physically separate the display.

▶ CLICK — Posture: how the device is being held — flat, folded, tabletop or book.

Rule number one for your next design review: ban the words “phone” and “tablet”.

Window is the space your app actually gets. Size class tells us which layout applies. Pane is a column of content. Hinge is where the screen bends, and where buttons must never die. And posture is how the user is holding it.
