Now let’s look at what designers actually own. They need to define layouts for different window sizes. But that doesn’t mean creating five independent Figma screens. They need to define how the experience transforms.

Imagine a messaging app. When the window is compact, we show the conversation list or the selected conversation. When the window becomes expanded, we show the list and the active conversation together.

The designer must also consider narrow split-screen: we shouldn’t sacrifice usability just because another app is open beside ours. Another important point is continuity. If the user has selected a conversation and the window changes, that selection should remain meaningful.

Finally, text needs comfortable reading dimensions. More width doesn’t automatically mean better readability.
